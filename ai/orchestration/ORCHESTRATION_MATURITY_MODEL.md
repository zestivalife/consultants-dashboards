# Purpose

The Enterprise Orchestration Maturity Model defines the capability maturity framework for assessing, benchmarking and continuously improving orchestration capabilities within the Enterprise AI Operating System (EAIOS).

The model provides a structured roadmap that enables organisations to evolve from basic workflow automation to fully autonomous, self-optimising enterprise orchestration.

The maturity model serves as the strategic measurement framework for enterprise orchestration excellence.

---

# Objectives

The Enterprise Orchestration Maturity Model SHALL:

- Assess orchestration capabilities.
- Benchmark organisational maturity.
- Identify capability gaps.
- Prioritise improvements.
- Standardise maturity assessments.
- Guide strategic investments.
- Measure enterprise progress.
- Enable continuous improvement.
- Support governance.
- Accelerate enterprise transformation.

---

# Scope

This standard applies to:

- Enterprise AI Platforms
- Multi-Agent Systems
- Workflow Platforms
- Automation Platforms
- AI Products
- Platform Engineering
- Enterprise Architecture
- AI Governance
- Operations
- DevOps

Every enterprise orchestration platform SHALL be periodically assessed using this maturity model.

---

# Maturity Principles

## Principle 1 — Continuous Evolution

Maturity SHALL be continuously improved rather than considered a one-time achievement.

---

## Principle 2 — Measurable Progress

Every maturity level SHALL have measurable criteria.

---

## Principle 3 — Business Alignment

Technical maturity SHALL align with business maturity.

---

## Principle 4 — Capability Driven

Assessment SHALL focus on enterprise capabilities rather than technology alone.

---

## Principle 5 — Governance First

Higher maturity SHALL require stronger governance.

---

## Principle 6 — Enterprise Scalability

Advanced maturity SHALL support enterprise-wide scalability.

---

# Maturity Assessment Framework

The maturity assessment SHALL evaluate:

- Architecture
- Engineering
- Workflows
- Agents
- Context
- Memory
- Knowledge
- Governance
- Security
- Operations
- Analytics
- Resilience

Every domain SHALL receive an independent maturity score.

---

# Maturity Level 1 — Initial

## Characteristics

- Manual orchestration
- Limited automation
- Ad-hoc workflows
- Minimal governance
- Reactive operations

## Capabilities

- Basic workflow execution
- Limited integrations
- Manual approvals
- Basic monitoring

---

# Maturity Level 2 — Managed

## Characteristics

- Standardised workflows
- Basic governance
- Defined processes
- Improved observability

## Capabilities

- Workflow Engine
- Tool Integration
- Basic Policies
- Execution Monitoring
- Standard Documentation

---

# Maturity Level 3 — Defined

## Characteristics

- Enterprise architecture established
- Multi-agent coordination
- Centralised governance
- Standard operating model

## Capabilities

- Intent Engine
- Execution Planner
- Agent Coordinator
- Context Management
- Policy Enforcement
- Analytics

---

# Maturity Level 4 — Optimised

## Characteristics

- Predictive orchestration
- Intelligent optimisation
- Adaptive execution
- Enterprise observability

## Capabilities

- Predictive Analytics
- Dynamic Context
- Intelligent Routing
- Self-Optimising Workflows
- Autonomous Recovery
- Enterprise Governance

---

# Maturity Level 5 — Autonomous

## Characteristics

- Self-learning orchestration
- Autonomous decision making
- Enterprise AI ecosystem
- Continuous optimisation
- Self-healing platform

## Capabilities

- Autonomous Planning
- Autonomous Agents
- Autonomous Recovery
- Continuous Learning
- AI Governance Automation
- Enterprise Intelligence

# Scoring Model

Every assessment SHALL include:

- Capability Score
- Maturity Level
- Gap Analysis
- Risk Rating
- Business Impact
- Improvement Priority

Overall maturity SHALL be calculated using weighted capability scores.

---

# Assessment Frequency

Assessments SHALL occur:

- Quarterly
- Before Major Releases
- After Significant Architectural Changes
- During Governance Reviews
- Following Major Incidents

# Quality Gates

An assessment SHALL fail validation if:

- Assessment criteria are incomplete.
- Required evidence is unavailable.
- Scoring methodology is inconsistent.
- Governance review is missing.
- Improvement roadmap is absent.
- Audit evidence is incomplete.
- Executive approval is unavailable.
