# Purpose

This workflow defines the enterprise standard for creating, maintaining, governing and communicating Product Roadmaps.

The Product Roadmap translates corporate strategy into a sequenced portfolio of initiatives, Epics and Features, ensuring product investment aligns with measurable business outcomes, customer value and engineering capacity.

Every product SHALL maintain an approved Product Roadmap.

---

# Objectives

- Align product investment with business strategy.
- Prioritise long-term initiatives.
- Improve portfolio visibility.
- Coordinate cross-functional planning.
- Balance innovation and maintenance.
- Enable AI-assisted roadmap planning.
- Improve forecasting accuracy.
- Maintain strategic traceability.

---

# Trigger Conditions

Execute this workflow when:

- Annual or quarterly planning begins.
- Corporate strategy changes.
- Product vision is updated.
- Market conditions shift.
- Customer demand changes significantly.
- Major acquisitions or partnerships occur.
- Portfolio reviews are scheduled.

---

# Required Inputs

The workflow SHALL NOT begin until the following artefacts are available:

- Corporate Strategy
- Product Vision
- OKRs
- Market Research
- Customer Insights
- Product Backlog
- Product Portfolio
- Engineering Capacity Forecast
- Financial Budget
- Risk Register

# Product Roadmap Principles

Every roadmap SHALL be:

- Strategy driven

- Outcome oriented
- Evidence based
- Flexible
- Transparent
- Measurable
- Continuously reviewed

# Workflow Stages

## Stage 1 — Strategic Alignment

Owner: Chief Product Officer

Activities:

- Review corporate objectives.
- Review market direction.
- Confirm product vision.
- Define investment themes.
- Align OKRs.

Output:

Strategic Alignment Report.

---

## Stage 2 — Portfolio Assessment

Owner: Product Strategy Office

Activities:

- Review active products.
- Evaluate investment balance.
- Review lifecycle stages.
- Assess portfolio performance.
- Identify opportunities.

Output:

Portfolio Assessment.

---

## Stage 3 — Initiative Planning

Owner: Product Managers

Activities:

- Define strategic initiatives.
- Prioritise Epics.
- Estimate investments.
- Define milestones.
- Identify dependencies.

Output:

Roadmap Draft.

---

## Stage 4 — Capacity & Financial Planning

Owner: Engineering Director

Activities:

- Forecast engineering capacity.
- Validate budget.
- Assess hiring needs.
- Review operational costs.
- Confirm delivery feasibility.

Output:

Capacity Forecast.

---

## Stage 5 — Risk Assessment

Owner: Enterprise Architect

Activities:

- Review technical risks.
- Review operational risks.
- Review market risks.
- Assess dependency risks.
- Define mitigation plans.

Output:

Roadmap Risk Assessment.

---

## Stage 6 — Governance Review

Owner: Portfolio Governance Board

Activities:

- Review strategic alignment.
- Validate investments.
- Confirm roadmap sequencing.
- Review dependencies.
- Recommend adjustments.

Output:

Governance Review Report.

---

## Stage 7 — Approval

Owner: Executive Steering Committee

Activities:

- Review roadmap.
- Approve investments.
- Confirm milestones.
- Approve publication.

Output:

Approved Product Roadmap.

---

## Stage 8 — Continuous Review

Owner: Product Strategy Office

Activities:

- Monitor progress.
- Update forecasts.
- Adjust priorities.
- Record changes.
- Publish revisions.

Output:

Roadmap Update.

# Roadmap Horizons

Every roadmap SHALL classify initiatives into:

- Now (0–3 Months)
- Next (3–9 Months)
- Later (9–24 Months)
- Future (24–36 Months)

Each horizon SHALL include confidence levels.

---

# Mandatory Roadmap Structure

Every roadmap SHALL include:

- Roadmap ID
- Product
- Vision
- Strategic Themes
- Business Objectives
- Investment Themes
- Strategic Initiatives
- Epics
- Features
- Releases
- Milestones
- Dependencies
- Capacity Forecast
- Budget Allocation
- Risk Register
- KPIs
- Success Metrics
- Stakeholders
- Approval History
- Revision History

---

# Investment Categories

The roadmap SHALL identify investment across:

- New Products
- New Features
- Customer Experience
- AI Capabilities
- Platform Modernisation
- Technical Debt
- Security
- Compliance
- Infrastructure
- Innovation
- Research
- Operational Excellence

---

# Roadmap Planning Standards

Planning SHALL include:

- Business value
- Customer outcomes
- Cost of Delay
- WSJF considerations
- Engineering capacity
- Financial constraints
- Technical dependencies
- Market timing

---

# Capacity Planning Standards

Roadmaps SHALL identify:

- Available engineering capacity
- AI-assisted capacity
- Vendor capacity
- Budget limits
- Skill gaps
- Hiring plans

---

# AI Planning Standards

AI MAY assist by:

- Forecasting delivery dates.
- Recommending priorities.
- Identifying dependency conflicts.
- Predicting delivery risks.
- Simulating roadmap scenarios.
- Estimating capacity utilisation.

Executive approval SHALL remain mandatory.

---

# Roadmap Governance

Every roadmap SHALL be reviewed:

- Monthly (operational review)
- Quarterly (strategic review)
- Annually (portfolio planning)

---

# Traceability Standards

Every roadmap SHALL reference:

- Corporate Strategy
- Vision
- OKRs
- Product Portfolio
- Strategic Initiatives
- Epics
- Features
- Releases
- Financial Plans
- Product Backlog

---

# Quality Gates

The workflow SHALL pause if:

- Strategy alignment is unclear.
- Capacity is unavailable.
- Budget is unapproved.
- Dependencies are unresolved.
- Executive approval is absent.

---

# Deliverables

Mandatory artefacts:

- Product Roadmap
- Portfolio Assessment
- Capacity Forecast
- Investment Plan
- Risk Assessment
- Governance Report

# Exit Criteria

The workflow completes when:

- Roadmap is approved.
- Investment priorities are confirmed.
- Capacity is allocated.
- Stakeholders are informed.
- Roadmap is published.
