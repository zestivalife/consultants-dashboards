# Purpose

This workflow defines the enterprise standard for creating, governing and managing Product Epics.

An Epic represents a significant business capability or strategic initiative that delivers measurable business value and is decomposed into Features and User Stories for implementation.

Every strategic initiative SHALL be represented by one or more approved Epics before engineering work begins.

---

# Objectives

- Translate business strategy into executable work.
- Organise large initiatives into manageable capabilities.
- Maintain alignment with business objectives.
- Standardise portfolio planning.
- Improve roadmap visibility.
- Enable AI-assisted planning.
- Maintain complete traceability.
- Support incremental delivery.

---

# Trigger Conditions

Execute this workflow when:

- A strategic initiative is approved.
- A new product capability is planned.
- A roadmap item enters delivery.
- Customer commitments require planning.
- Product investment decisions are approved.

---

# Required Inputs

The workflow SHALL NOT begin until the following artefacts are available:

- Business Strategy
- Product Vision
- Approved PRD
- Product Roadmap
- Market Research
- Customer Insights
- Enterprise Objectives
- Investment Priorities

---

# Epic Principles

Every Epic SHALL be:

- Business outcome focused
- Customer centred
- Strategically aligned
- Measurable
- Independently valuable
- Traceable
- AI-readable
- Incrementally deliverable

# Workflow Stages

## Stage 1 — Strategic Alignment

Owner: Product Strategy Office

Activities:

- Review corporate objectives.
- Review product strategy.
- Define strategic outcomes.
- Identify value streams.
- Align investment priorities.

Output:

Strategic Alignment Report.

---

## Stage 2 — Business Capability Definition

Owner: Product Architect

Activities:

- Define business capability.
- Identify target users.
- Define expected outcomes.
- Define success measures.
- Identify operational impact.

Output:

Business Capability Definition.

---

## Stage 3 — Epic Definition

Owner: Product Owner

Activities:

- Define Epic scope.
- Identify major deliverables.
- Define business value.
- Estimate investment.
- Identify constraints.

Output:

Draft Epic.

---

## Stage 4 — Feature Decomposition

Owner: Product Owner

Activities:

- Identify Features.
- Define implementation sequence.
- Identify dependencies.
- Prioritise Features.
- Validate incremental delivery.

Output:

Feature Catalogue.

---

## Stage 5 — Technical Review

Owner: Enterprise Architect

Activities:

- Validate architecture.
- Review implementation feasibility.
- Identify technical dependencies.
- Assess complexity.
- Validate scalability.

Output:

Technical Review Report.

---

## Stage 6 — Roadmap Planning

Owner: Product Strategy Office

Activities:

- Position Epic on roadmap.
- Define milestones.
- Allocate releases.
- Review capacity.
- Coordinate cross-team dependencies.

Output:

Roadmap Update.

---

## Stage 7 — Approval

Owner: Product Steering Committee

Activities:

- Review business value.
- Review investment.
- Review risks.
- Approve Epic.
- Register Epic.

Output:

Approved Epic.

---

## Stage 8 — Continuous Governance

Owner: Product Owner

Activities:

- Monitor progress.
- Review value delivery.
- Update forecasts.
- Adjust scope.
- Close Epic upon completion.

Output:

Epic Status Report.

- OKRs

# AI Planning Standards

Every Epic SHALL:

- Be semantically structured.
- Use deterministic headings.
- Support automated decomposition.
- Enable AI backlog planning.

---

# Quality Gates

The workflow SHALL pause if:

- Business value is undefined.
- Success metrics are missing.
- Scope is ambiguous.
- Dependencies are unresolved.
- Roadmap alignment is incomplete.
- Required approvals are missing.
