# Purpose

The Enterprise AI Governance Standard establishes the mandatory governance model, organisational responsibilities, decision rights, policy framework and oversight mechanisms for every capability within the Enterprise AI Operating System (EAIOS).

The objective is to ensure AI systems remain trustworthy, compliant, explainable, auditable and aligned with enterprise strategy throughout their lifecycle.

Governance SHALL be embedded into every business, engineering and operational activity.

---

# Objectives

The Enterprise AI Governance Standard SHALL:

- Establish enterprise-wide AI governance.
- Define decision ownership.
- Standardise governance processes.
- Ensure regulatory compliance.
- Improve accountability.
- Enable policy-driven operations.
- Support responsible AI.
- Strengthen enterprise risk management.
- Improve audit readiness.
- Enable continuous governance.

# Governance Principles

## Principle 1 — Accountability

Every AI capability SHALL have a designated business and technical owner.

---

## Principle 2 — Transparency

Governance decisions SHALL be documented and auditable.

---

## Principle 3 — Responsibility

AI systems SHALL operate within approved ethical and regulatory boundaries.

---

## Principle 4 — Policy Driven

Enterprise policies SHALL govern AI behaviour.

---

## Principle 5 — Continuous Oversight

Governance SHALL continue throughout the AI lifecycle.

---

## Principle 6 — Risk-Based Governance

Governance intensity SHALL increase according to business risk.

---

## Principle 7 — Explainability

Governance decisions SHALL be explainable.

---

## Principle 8 — Auditability

Every governance decision SHALL generate immutable audit evidence.

---

## Principle 9 — Human Oversight

Critical business decisions SHALL support human review.

---

## Principle 10 — Continuous Improvement

Governance policies SHALL evolve through operational learning.

# Quality Gates

Governance approval SHALL fail if:

- Required approvals are missing.
- Policy compliance cannot be demonstrated.
- Risks exceed approved thresholds.
- Audit evidence is incomplete.
- Regulatory obligations are unmet.
- Governance ownership is undefined.
- Exception approvals are absent.
