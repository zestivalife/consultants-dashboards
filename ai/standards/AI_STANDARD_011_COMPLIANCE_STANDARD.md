# Purpose

The Enterprise AI Compliance Standard establishes the mandatory regulatory, legal, contractual and internal compliance requirements governing every capability within the Enterprise AI Operating System (EAIOS).

The objective is to ensure enterprise AI systems continuously comply with applicable laws, regulations, contractual obligations, organisational policies and recognised industry standards while providing complete evidence of compliance.

Compliance SHALL be continuous, measurable and auditable.

---

# Objectives

The Enterprise AI Compliance Standard SHALL:

- Establish enterprise compliance governance.
- Standardise regulatory controls.
- Enable continuous compliance.
- Reduce regulatory risk.
- Improve audit readiness.
- Support global regulatory obligations.
- Automate compliance monitoring.
- Improve organisational accountability.
- Maintain enterprise trust.
- Enable compliance-by-design.

# Compliance Principles

## Principle 1 — Compliance by Design

Compliance SHALL be integrated into architecture, engineering and operations.

---

## Principle 2 — Continuous Compliance

Compliance SHALL be continuously monitored rather than periodically verified.

---

## Principle 3 — Evidence-Based Governance

Every compliance assertion SHALL be supported by verifiable evidence.

---

## Principle 4 — Policy Alignment

Enterprise controls SHALL align with approved organisational policies.

---

## Principle 5 — Regulatory Traceability

Every regulatory obligation SHALL map to enterprise controls.

---

## Principle 6 — Risk-Based Compliance

Compliance activities SHALL prioritise high-risk capabilities.

---

## Principle 7 — Automation First

Compliance verification SHOULD be automated wherever possible.

---

## Principle 8 — Audit Readiness

Enterprise systems SHALL maintain audit-ready evidence at all times.

---

## Principle 9 — Transparency

Compliance status SHALL be visible to authorised stakeholders.

---

## Principle 10 — Continuous Improvement

Compliance controls SHALL evolve as regulations change.

# Quality Gates

Compliance approval SHALL fail if:

- Mandatory controls are missing.
- Evidence cannot be verified.
- Regulatory mappings are incomplete.
- Policy compliance cannot be demonstrated.
- Critical audit findings remain unresolved.
- Exception approvals are absent.
- Compliance monitoring is unavailable.
