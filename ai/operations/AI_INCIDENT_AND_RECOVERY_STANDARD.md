# AI_INCIDENT_AND_RECOVERY_STANDARD

## Purpose

Establish the enterprise standard for detecting, managing, resolving and recovering from operational incidents while minimising business disruption, restoring normal service operations quickly and ensuring continuous organisational learning.

This standard governs the complete incident lifecycle from detection through recovery, root cause analysis and continuous improvement.

---

## Objectives

- Standardise enterprise incident management.
- Minimise business impact.
- Restore services rapidly.
- Establish structured incident response.
- Govern incident escalation.
- Standardise recovery procedures.
- Perform root cause analysis.
- Capture operational learnings.
- Prevent incident recurrence.
- Improve enterprise resilience.

---

## Scope

This standard applies to:

- Production Platforms
- Enterprise Applications
- APIs
- Microservices
- Infrastructure
- Cloud Services
- AI Services
- Databases
- Integrations
- Business Workflows
- Customer-facing Systems
- Third-party Services

---

## Incident & Recovery Principles

### Customer First

Incident response shall prioritise customer experience and business continuity.

---

### Rapid Response

Incidents shall be acknowledged and investigated immediately according to severity.

---

### Evidence-Based Investigation

Incident decisions shall be supported by operational evidence and verified facts.

---

### Coordinated Response

All incident stakeholders shall work through a structured command and communication model.

---

### Service Restoration First

The primary objective is to restore business service before pursuing permanent fixes.

---

### Continuous Learning

Every significant incident shall result in documented learnings and improvement actions.

---

## Incident Classification

### Critical (P1)

- Complete service outage
- Major security event
- Critical business disruption
- Enterprise-wide customer impact

Immediate response required.

---

### High (P2)

- Major feature unavailable
- Significant customer impact
- High operational risk

Rapid response required.

---

### Medium (P3)

- Limited operational impact
- Partial service degradation
- Workaround available

Planned resolution required.

---

### Low (P4)

- Minor issue
- Cosmetic defect
- No business impact

Scheduled resolution.

---

## Incident Lifecycle

Every incident shall follow:

1. Detection
2. Logging
3. Classification
4. Assessment
5. Assignment
6. Investigation
7. Containment
8. Service Restoration
9. Recovery Validation
10. Root Cause Analysis
11. Post-Incident Review
12. Closure

---

## Incident Detection

Incidents may be detected through:

- Monitoring Systems
- Operational Alerts
- Customer Reports
- Support Teams
- Security Events
- AI Monitoring
- Business KPI Degradation
- Automated Detection

---

## Incident Assessment

Assessment shall determine:

- Business Impact
- Customer Impact
- Service Criticality
- Security Impact
- Compliance Impact
- Financial Impact
- Operational Risk

---

## Incident Response

Response activities include:

- Incident Declaration
- Incident Ownership
- Stakeholder Notification
- Investigation
- Mitigation
- Escalation
- Communication
- Recovery Coordination

---

## Major Incident Management

Major incidents shall include:

- Incident Commander
- Technical Lead
- Communications Lead
- Business Representative
- Executive Sponsor (when required)

Major incidents shall follow dedicated communication and governance procedures.

---

## Escalation Management

Escalation shall occur when:

- Business impact increases.
- Customer impact expands.
- SLA is at risk.
- Recovery exceeds expected timelines.
- Security or regulatory risks emerge.

Escalation paths shall be predefined and documented.

---

## Recovery Management

Recovery activities include:

- Service Restoration
- Infrastructure Recovery
- Database Recovery
- AI Service Recovery
- Business Workflow Validation
- Customer Validation
- Operational Verification

Recovery shall be validated before incident closure.

---

## Root Cause Analysis

Every major incident shall undergo Root Cause Analysis (RCA).

RCA shall identify:

- Immediate Cause
- Contributing Factors
- Systemic Weaknesses
- Process Gaps
- Preventive Actions

Root Cause Analysis shall focus on system improvement rather than individual blame.

---

## Post-Incident Review

Reviews shall evaluate:

- Timeline of Events
- Response Effectiveness
- Recovery Performance
- Communication Quality
- Customer Impact
- Lessons Learned
- Improvement Opportunities

---

## Communication Management

Communication shall include:

- Incident Declaration
- Progress Updates
- Business Impact
- Estimated Recovery Time
- Recovery Confirmation
- Closure Notification

Communication shall be timely, accurate and transparent.

---

## Incident Metrics

Track:

- Incident Volume
- Incident Severity Distribution
- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)
- Mean Time to Respond
- Mean Time to Recover (MTTR)
- Service Restoration Time
- Recurring Incidents
- Root Cause Completion Rate
- SLA Compliance
- Customer Impact

---

## Roles & Responsibilities

Incident Management teams shall:

- Coordinate incident response.
- Manage incident communications.
- Restore affected services.
- Validate recovery.
- Conduct Root Cause Analysis.
- Document operational learnings.
- Track improvement actions.

## Continuous Improvement

Incident Management shall continuously improve through:

- Post-Incident Reviews
- Root Cause Analysis
- Automation
- Operational Reviews
- Recovery Testing
- Process Optimisation

---

## Final Principle

Incident and Recovery Management exists to restore business services quickly, minimise customer impact, learn from operational failures and continuously strengthen enterprise resilience.
